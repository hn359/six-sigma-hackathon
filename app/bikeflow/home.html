About

A control-chart dispatcher for bikeshare vans

Bikeshare stations run empty or full at rush hour, and the rebalancing van is often sent blindly. This tool watches every station at every reading. It sends the van when a station behaves unusually for that time of day or leaves its healthy range, and it plans how many bikes each station should start with the next morning.

User: the dispatcher who runs one rebalancing van and a reserve depot of spare bikes.

For the dispatcher

How a dispatcher uses it

  1. Open the Tool tab. The map console is on top, the BikeFlow analysis sections below.
  2. Pick a city and a day: Event day (a day with planted problems), Week 2 · policy (the van runs on our rules) or Week 1 · no van (the baseline), then a weekday.
  3. Press Play or drag the time bar. One reading per step; the speed menu sets how fast. Amber bands under the bar mark the rush hours, and a Rush hour badge shows while the replay is inside one. Every section below has the same bar and follows the top one; only the top one can be dragged.
  4. Read the map. Each station card shows bikes docked out of its docks and its status (in control, or the rule that fired). The ranked list on the right puts the station that needs the van most at the top; the van drives to the stop it makes.
  5. Scroll the BikeFlow sections:
    • 01 Station status: how full each station is now, against the healthy range and a normal day.
    • 02 Today at each station: bikes through the day; red dots are unusual readings.
    • 03 Rider flow: which routes riders took, last hour or today.
    • 04 Redistribution: which bikes the van should move now, from where to where.
    • 05 Tomorrow's bike plan: the 10 PM plan that sets each station for the 6 AM opening.
    • 06 Control chart: the station's I-MR chart with the eight tests.
    • 07 Validation: whether the chart caught the problems we planted.
  6. One flag means look; repeated flags mean act. Small counts make the chart flag more often than 3σ suggests, so a single dot is a prompt to check, not proof.
The decisions

What the tool decides

Every reading: does the van go, where, and how many bikes

  1. For each station, compare its rider net flow with what is usual for that time slot.
  2. The station triggers if a control-chart rule fires, or if it is outside 20%–80% of its docks (a safety net for slow drifts).
  3. The van moves the station back toward its expected level: at most 10 bikes, one stop per reading, the station furthest from its expected level first.
  4. Bikes come from (or go to) the station with the most to spare on the other side, then the reserve depot.

Every night: how many bikes each station starts with

From week 1's average 06:00–10:00 net flow, the station riders leave most starts at 80% of its docks, the one they arrive at most at 20%, and the rest in proportion between. In Collegetown that gives starts of 15, 4, 16, 8 and 9 bikes (S01–S05). The plan is only published once week 1 is fully observed.

Statistics

The statistics

WhatHow
Chart pointrider net flow in a slot (change in bikes minus the van's move) minus the usual net flow for that slot over the baseline week. This removes the normal rush-hour drain, so only unexplained variation is charted.
Control limits (I-MR)σ = MR̄ / 1.128; limits = centre ± 3σ; moving-range UCL = 3.267 MR̄. Learned from week 1 only (Phase I), then fixed.
Rules that send the vanWestern Electric rules 1–4 (beyond 3σ; 2 of 3 beyond 2σ; 4 of 5 beyond 1σ; 8 in a row on one side), or the station outside 20%–80% of its docks.
Rules on the BikeFlow chartthe course's eight tests plus a moving range above its limit. These are shown for analysis; the van currently acts on rules 1–4 above, so a dot below can appear without a van visit.
Weekly reviewa p-chart of each day's share of out-of-range readings: week 1 sets p̄ and the limits, week 2 days are judged against them.
Rush hoursa slot is rush when the bikes riders move in or out of all stations is at least 1.5× the day's average (week 1); rush slots less than an hour apart are joined. Collegetown: 07:00–09:15 and 16:00–18:30.

Assumptions: chart points roughly independent and normal; readings within a day are correlated, so limits are a little tight; seven baseline days per slot average.

Data

Where the data comes from

Our simulator (data/simulate.py) plays out a small bikeshare city with a fixed random seed, so every file can be rebuilt exactly.

  1. Riders: at each reading, the number of rentals at a station is a Poisson draw with a rate for that station and time of day (higher at rush hour).
  2. Trips: each rider rides to another station, picked with weights for that time of day: homes to campus and offices in the morning, back in the evening.
  3. Limits: an empty station loses the rental; a full one sends the rider on to the emptiest station with room.
  4. Three files per city: week 1 with no van (the baseline), week 2 with the van running our rules, and an event day with two planted problems (a slow drain and a crowd) to test the chart.

The commuter cities improve by about three quarters. Waterfront gets worse: its leisure demand has no morning commute, so the 80/20 overnight start built from 06:00–10:00 flow does not fit it. On the Collegetown event day the BikeFlow chart flags the slow drain at 11:15 and the crowd at 18:15, inside both planted windows.

Limits

Known limits

  • Simulated data only; demand rates are invented and tuned, not fitted to a real system.
  • One van, one stop per reading, no travel time or van capacity.
  • Same demand every day: no weekends, weather or semester effects.
  • The history is learned once from week 1 and never updated.
  • Rush hours use net-flow activity, not rentals (the readings have no rental counts).
Reproduce

Run it yourself

git clone https://github.com/hn359/six-sigma-hackathon.git && cd six-sigma-hackathon
python3 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r app/requirements.txt -r app/bikeflow/requirements.txt
cd app/frontend && npm ci && npm run build && cd ../..
.venv/bin/python -m uvicorn app.bikeflow.combined:app --port 8000

Then open http://localhost:8000. The API's interactive docs are at /docs. Code, tests and the full technical README are in the team repository.
